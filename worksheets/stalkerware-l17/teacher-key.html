Teacher key

Stalkerware, lesson 17. Timing, answers, and what to do when something comes up.

Not for students
90 min · 2–5 students

Your role

  • Start the timer for each task and say “next task”. The worksheet tells students what to do and what to post.
  • Read the chat and check posted answers against this key. Answer with “OK” or “check number 3 again”. Let students find the reason themselves.
  • Keep language corrections for the end of the lesson (see Language, page 2).

Before class — 15 to 20 minutes

  • Do Tasks 1, 2 and 3 yourself and check them against this key (10 min).
  • On your own phone, open the places listed in Task 4 (5 min). Menu names depend on the brand and the Android or iOS version. Know where the search bar in Settings is.
  • Send the worksheet 5 minutes before the lesson and have a timer ready. Nothing else to prepare.

Attendance

  • 2 students: no changes. Both do every task. Give the extras to the faster student.
  • 3–5 students: no changes. For situation D and the extras, give each student one question and let the others reply to it. In Task 3 you can give each student two rows of the table, then everyone posts and compares.
  • Late student: send the sheet, tell them the current task number. They skim the text and join in.
  • No phone, or a work phone: the student does the account check at the end of Task 4.

If a student finds something

  • Students post only counts, never app names. If someone writes that they found something, reply “noted, we continue” and do not ask for details in the group.
  • A “Look” is not proof. VPN apps, password managers, accessibility tools, smartwatch apps and work-profile apps often appear in these lists.
  • Do not ask anyone to delete anything during the lesson. After class, in private, point to stopstalkerware.org. In Poland: Niebieska Linia, 800 120 002 (free, 24 hours).

Language

  • Main language: present simple for describing how things work (Tasks 1–3), imperatives and “Don't …” (Task 5), “because” for reasons, “First / Then / After that” for order. A2 students can build their Task 5 reply from the chosen options.
  • Do not correct during Task 4. Collect 3 or 4 mistakes that repeat in the chat and show them, without names, in the last 5 minutes.

4T“On an iPhone, they sometimes need only the email and password … not the phone.”
5F“It often has no icon.” The text says often, not always.
6F“Those apps are visible, and the people who use the phone know about them.”
7NGThe text gives no numbers.

Task 2 — answers

D, question 1. It is not stalkerware: the sharing is visible and it started with consent. But it is still a problem, because Anna does not agree to it any more. Accept “knows” and “agreed” as Y or N if the student gives a reason.

D, question 2. Anna should stop sharing. iPhone: Find My → People, or Settings → Privacy & Security → Safety Check. Android: Google Maps → profile picture → Location sharing. She can also tell Tom.

+ ExtraNo single right answer. Accept any reason, for example: the teenager does not know, so trust can break; or a parent is responsible for a young child.

Task 4 — what normal looks like

There is no key. Students post counts like “Android – 6 checks, 1 to look at”. Use this list only if a student asks what a result means.

SystemUsually normal
AndroidPlay Protect is on. The Accessibility and Device admin lists are empty, or contain tools the student knows (screen reader, password manager, work app). Most apps are “Not allowed” to install unknown apps; a browser or file manager may be “Allowed” if the student installs files by hand. Notification access: smartwatch or car apps.
iPhoneSafety Check lists only people and apps the student chose. The device list shows only their own devices. Two-Factor Authentication is on. No “Profiles” section, or only a work or school profile. Find My → People shows only people the student chose.

Task 5 — answers

Good advice: B, C, E, F, G. Not good: A, D, H.

  • A. Removing the app can warn the person who watches, and it deletes the evidence. Telling him can make the situation more dangerous.
  • D. Asking him also warns him.
  • H. “Spy detectors” from random websites can be scams or spyware themselves.
  • F. Good, but changing passwords can also show the person that the friend knows. This is why E (a support organisation) is on the list: it helps to plan the order. Accept any order, as long as the reset is not first.
Model reply, B1–B2Don't reset your phone yet, because the app can warn him and you can lose the evidence. First, don't message anyone about this on that phone. Then use a different phone or computer to look for help, for example on stopstalkerware.org. Write down the names of the apps and the dates. After that, contact a support organisation or the police. When you have a plan, change your passwords from the safe device and turn on two-factor authentication. If you feel unsafe, call for help immediately.
Model reply, A2Don't reset the phone. Use another phone. Write down the app names. Call the police or a support organisation. Then change your passwords.

Task 6

No key. Check the pattern: This week I will turn on two-factor authentication. (will + base verb).

The menu paths in Task 4 and the advice in Task 5 come from published guides (Google, Apple, Coalition Against Stalkerware). Menus change with each phone brand and version, so test them on your own phone before the lesson.
